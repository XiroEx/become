## Synthesized patterns

### 1. Ad formats present

- **Feature-list story** (IMG_7983–7986). One hub per frame, headline + 4–6 arrow bullets, phone bleeding off one edge. Four frames = a whole product tour. Fast to produce, information-dense, low emotional pull.
- **Feature hero** (7987, 7988). One claim, big type, one hero visual. Highest production value, lowest information density.
- **"App Tip" how-to sequence** (7991–7998). The strongest thing in the library. A branded pill badge opens it, each frame teaches one tap, hand-drawn annotations point at the exact control, and the last frame is the CTA. This is education as advertising and it earns the swipe.
- **Themed recurring series** (7999 "MILESTONE WEDNESDAY"). A named weekly slot that turns ad inventory into a habit for the audience.
- **Modality carousel** (Ladder, 8010–8014). Cover states the problem and enumerates N solutions; each following slide is one solution with an identical lower-third lockup. Rhythmic and extremely scannable.
- **Social-proof-as-screenshot** (8003, 8004). Show a real thread, let members' words do the claiming.

Notably absent: memes, talking-head UGC, before/after transformations, testimonial cards, and any pricing/offer creative. Both brands sell the *mechanism*, not a promise.

### 2. Layout systems

- **Text-left / phone-right-bleed** is STNDRD's workhorse (7983–7986, 8002, 8005). The phone deliberately runs off-canvas — it implies "there's more app than fits", and it dodges the need for a polished full-device render.
- **Headline-top / phone-bottom** for heroes (7988, 7994, 8006–8008); **headline-bottom** when the screenshot itself is the argument (7993, 7997). The rule that emerges: *put the type on the side of the frame the eye should land on second.*
- **Multi-phone tilt/fan** (7987, 7994, 7995, 8007, 8010). Two to six devices at slight rotations, overlapping. Six phones (Ladder cover) reads as "the app does six things" before a single word is parsed.
- **Cut-out floating UI chips** (7991, 7997, 8002, 8004). A control is lifted out of the screenshot and floated over the background with a shadow. Cheap to make, and it magnifies a 40px control to hero size without a zoom-in.
- **Hand-drawn annotation** (7992, 7993, 7995, 7996, 8002). White marker ellipses, arrows and underlines. Human, un-corporate, and unambiguous about where to tap.
- **Persistent footer lockup** (7982–7987). Tagline + both store badges pinned to the bottom. Consistency = brand.

### 3. Color and type

- STNDRD: a single electric-blue → black radial gradient carried across ~25 assets, with visible film grain. One accent (#0A6BFF-ish blue) that matches the in-app primary button color, so the ad and the product feel like one object. Type is a wide condensed grotesque, all-caps, tight leading, 3–4 line stacks, occasionally two-tone (white/blue) to split a headline into rhythm.
- Ladder: dark green→black, heavy condensed all-caps with a hard drop shadow, one glass-morphic pill as the only chrome. Their green sits close to Become's green.
- Both are dark-first, high-contrast, single-accent systems. Neither uses more than two type weights per frame.

### 4. CTA styles

- Soft, low-friction, never price-led: "Tap to learn more →", a bare `→`, "🔗 DOWNLOAD THE STNDRD APP ✔" as a native-looking link sticker, and standing store badges.
- The strongest CTA in the set is structural, not verbal — the how-to sequence ends on the download frame, so the reader arrives at the CTA already having learned something.
- Ladder's CTA is the caption, not the creative. The creative's job is to stop the scroll.

### 5. Premium vs. cheap

**Reads premium:** one gradient system reused everywhere; real photography with real hands and real food (Ladder 8011–8014); the moody gym hero (7987); a quantified honesty chip ("Est. Accuracy" with fewer bars for weaker methods) — self-deprecation is a luxury signal; generous negative space around a single claim; consistent footer lockup.

**Reads cheap:** the leaked empty states (8006's 0 cal / 0 activity, 8007's grid of greyed "Not Achieved" medals, 8008's "14,637th") — nothing undercuts an aspirational ad faster than a demo account that hasn't done anything; the blurred-out label in 8001, which signals "we shipped a screenshot we weren't allowed to show"; the white-corner gradient slip in 8005; body-copy-only frames with no headline (7989); six-bullet walls that no one reads in a 5-second story; scattered decorative emoji/sticker icons (8003) that add nothing.

### 6. Fit / clash with Become

**Aligned:** dark-first single-accent gradient (our green maps 1:1 onto their blue); mechanism-over-promise selling, which is exactly "Evidence, not vibes"; teaching sequences, which suit a coach-led product; honest capability labeling (Ladder's accuracy chip); real-world photography for AI features that happen in the physical world (camera, plate, barcode).

**Clashes:** the gamification/XP/leaderboard vein (7999, 8006–8008) is competitive-ranking energy that pulls against "empowering, not preachy" — Become's streaks and The Becoming are self-referential, not a ladder you climb over other people; "DON'T BE A BUM" is shame-based and violates our no-body-shaming line; the six-bullet feature dump contradicts "simple · sleek"; STNDRD leans on athlete-celebrity aesthetics we cannot and should not fake.

## Steal this

1. **The "App Tip" sequence as a repeatable unit.** Badge → 3–4 one-tap-per-frame teaching steps → payoff frame → CTA frame. Become equivalents that write themselves: how to log a set with PR history, how to shoot one photo and itemize a plate, how to start LIVE rep counting, how to build a session with the AI generator, how to read your weekly recap.
2. **Hand-drawn annotation over product screenshots.** One white marker ellipse or arrow per frame, pointing at the exact control. Warm, unmistakable, and it makes a static capture feel guided. Pairs perfectly with our dummy-account capture pipeline.
3. **Cut-out floating UI chips.** Lift one control out of a `webapp/public/screenshots/v2/` capture, float it with a soft shadow. Ideal for the water tile, the streak ring, the mood selector, the rep counter — small elements that deserve hero treatment.
4. **Phone bleeding off the frame edge.** Text on one side, device running off the other. Implies depth, cheap to compose, and safe on story/square/landscape alike.
5. **The modality carousel** (Ladder's exact structure). Become's version: "FIVE WAYS BECOME LOGS YOUR DAY" → photo plate · barcode · set logging · weight · mood. Identical lower-third lockup on every slide. Slots directly into the Remotion compositions.
6. **A single accent carried across every asset**, matched to the in-app primary. Green #16a34a / #22c55e as the gradient, violet reserved strictly for AI/Mind frames, gold reserved for streaks/Becoming. Never three accents in one frame.
7. **A quantified-honesty chip.** Ladder's "Est. Accuracy" is the most trustworthy element in the whole library. Become can label AI outputs as estimates in-creative — it says "evidence, not vibes" without making a claim we'd have to defend.
8. **Real hands, real food, real rooms** for photo/barcode/LIVE features. The physical-world shot plus a screen overlay beats a floating device render.
9. **Two-tone headline stacks** (white line over accent line) to give an all-caps stack rhythm without a second typeface. Geist handles this well.
10. **A named recurring slot.** Not "Milestone Wednesday" — something Become-native and non-competitive, e.g. a weekly recap teaser tied to The Becoming (kept to one section/mention, never the headline theme).
11. **Structural CTA placement.** End every sequence on the same low-friction frame: email magic link, no credit card, free today. That is a genuinely strong offer and needs no invented pricing.

## Avoid this

1. **Any frame containing an empty or zero state.** STNDRD shipped three. Our capture pipeline must gate on it: no 0-value rings, no greyed "not achieved" grids, no "you haven't created anything yet", no `(beta)` label, no visible bug.
2. **Blurring something out.** If a capture needs redaction it should not ship. Re-capture from the dummy account instead.
3. **Leaderboards and rank-vs-others framing.** "14,637th" is a demotivator, and comparison-driven marketing clashes with empowering-not-preachy. Compare the user to their own last week, not to strangers.
4. **Shame-based taglines.** "DON'T BE A BUM" is off-limits, along with every before/after body-shaming construction.
5. **Six-bullet feature dumps.** Cap at three bullets per frame, ideally one claim. "Simple · sleek" has to be legible in the creative itself.
6. **Body-copy-only frames** with no headline (7989). Every asset earns a headline.
7. **Template slips** — the inverted white corner in 8005. One gradient spec, checked per render.
8. **Decorative sticker clutter** (floating bells and emoji). If an element does not point at something or state something, cut it.
9. **Celebrity/athlete-physique borrowing.** Jon Don's credibility is coaching, not a mass-monster aesthetic — and per the hard constraints, no personal camera-roll photos of the coach.
10. **Any fabricated number.** No like-counts, member counts, XP, results, testimonials, or "N users" copy. Where competitors lean on social proof screenshots, Become leans on product mechanism until real, permissioned proof exists.
11. **Leading with The Becoming.** It is design inspiration and at most one section or mention per campaign — never the headline theme.
