# Script Archetypes

Six structures. The SKILL.md carries five complete worked scripts (A through F). This file gives
the structural skeleton of each, when to reach for it, how it fails, and the sixth archetype's
long variant.

---

## 1. Mechanism demo

**Payoff:** the thing works, visibly, on screen.
**Band:** 15-30s. **CTA:** send.

Skeleton:
1. Mid-action open. The mechanism is already happening (0.0-1.5s).
2. The mechanism named once, on screen (1.5-4s).
3. The result, unedited (4-8s).
4. The second-order benefit: what it saved you (8-14s).
5. Loop-close on the opening frame plus the send CTA.

**Fails when:** the demo is explained before it is shown; the screen recording is too small to
read at 390px; the mechanism is claimed rather than filmed.
**Become fits:** plate photo itemization, barcode scan, a set logged in LIVE mode with last
session's numbers underneath, PR history surfacing on the set row.

## 2. App Tip sequence

**Payoff:** the viewer can now do the thing themselves.
**Band:** 30-45s, or a 4-6 slide carousel. **CTA:** save or keyword.

Skeleton:
1. Badge or pill opens it, plus the promise in four words.
2. One tap per beat, with an annotation on the exact control.
3. Three to five steps, no more.
4. The payoff frame: the finished state.
5. CTA frame, after the learning.

This is the strongest structure in `marketing/inspo-analysis.md`. Hand-drawn annotation over a
real capture is the specific detail that makes it work: warm, unambiguous, cheap to produce.

**Fails when:** two taps share a beat; the annotation points at the wrong control; the steps are
narrated but not shown.
**Become fits:** logging a set with PR history, generating a session against today's equipment,
swapping an exercise, shooting a clean plate photo, reading the week strip.

## 3. Misconception flip

**Payoff:** the viewer was told a wrong rule and now has the right one.
**Band:** 21-34s. **CTA:** send.

Skeleton:
1. State the misconception flatly, no preamble.
2. Name the real constraint underneath it.
3. Show the product doing the thing that dissolves it.
4. One line of proof from the screen.
5. Loop-close plus send CTA that names the recipient.

**Fails when:** the flip is an opinion with nothing behind it; the misconception is a straw man;
the tone slides into scolding.
**Become fits:** "logging is not the hard part, five apps was"; "you do not need a perfect week";
"tracking does not require a food scale."

**Hard line:** attack the situation, never the person. "You were sold a complicated system" is
fine. "You are lazy" is banned.

## 4. Day in the plan

**Payoff:** this fits a real week, including a bad one.
**Band:** 30-45s. **CTA:** keyword.

Skeleton:
1. The real constraint in frame one: minutes available, equipment present.
2. The plan adapting to it, on screen.
3. The session happening, compressed.
4. The week updating itself.
5. Loop-close plus keyword CTA.

**Fails when:** it becomes a vlog; the constraint is unrealistic (a perfect empty gym); the
product only appears at the end.
**Become fits:** the AI generator, schedule and training days, phase progression, training around
a trip.

## 5. Coach answer

**Payoff:** a real question answered with a reason, not a rule.
**Band:** 30-45s. **CTA:** send, or a reply prompt on a real question.

Skeleton:
1. Jon mid-answer, no greeting. The question stated as the viewer would say it.
2. The wrong default named.
3. The rule, plus the reason behind the rule.
4. One product insert showing the app already handles the mechanical part.
5. The reassurance line, then loop-close.

**Fails when:** Jon speaks product marketing copy; the answer is a platitude; a client story is
invented to carry it.
**Become fits:** missed sessions, starting weight selection, soreness, deloads, plateau weeks,
why a phase changes.

**Hard lines:** never invent a client, a client result, or a personal anecdote. Injury, medical,
and pregnancy questions get the referral answer (see `coach-brand-voice`).

## 6. Recap reveal

**Payoff:** your week, written back to you.
**Band:** 15-25s. **CTA:** save.

Skeleton:
1. Open on the recap card already moving. No logo.
2. One stat per beat, all of them user-generated.
3. The written recap paragraph.
4. Next week's first session.
5. Loop-close plus save CTA.

**Fails when:** the numbers imply an outcome ("down 4 lbs"); the chart is empty or single-point;
The Becoming becomes the theme instead of one beat.
**Become fits:** Sunday recap, mood next to volume, strength trend reading.

**Long variant, 45s teaching cut:**

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0.0-1.5 | Recap card opening, overlay "Sunday" |
| 1.5-8 | Three logged stats, one per beat |
| 8-16 | The written recap paragraph, read aloud |
| 16-26 | What to actually do with it: one thing to keep, one thing to change |
| 26-36 | Mood trend beside training volume, the pattern explained |
| 36-42 | Next week's first session on screen |
| 42-45 | Loop-close on the recap card, save CTA |

Only shoot the long variant when the account already has watch-time evidence that 45s holds.

---

## Choosing between them

| If the ask is | Use |
|---|---|
| "Show that it works" | Mechanism demo |
| "Teach people how to use X" | App Tip sequence |
| "People think X about fitness apps" | Misconception flip |
| "Show it fitting a real life" | Day in the plan |
| "Jon should say something" | Coach answer |
| "Show what they get on Sunday" | Recap reveal, capped at one slot per week |

Never blend two archetypes in one video. A demo that turns into a coach answer halfway loses both
payoffs and the loop-close stops working.
