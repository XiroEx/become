# Competitive Alternatives Map

What a best-fit user does if Become does not exist. Written from their point of view, not ours.
Every "where it fails" line is an `[assumption, unvalidated]` until it comes from a real review,
DM, or interview. `competitor-analysis` review mining is the cheapest way to convert them.

## The five

### 1. Nothing, plus the Notes app

**Why they pick it.** Free, zero setup, already on the phone, no account, no commitment.
**Real strength.** Frictionless. It never fails to open, never asks for an email, never nags.
**Where it fails them.** No structure, so every session starts with a decision. No memory across
weeks, so progression is guesswork. No feedback, so there is no evidence anything is working.
**What we say.** Not that notes are bad. That the week gets decided for you: "A coach's program,
with the week already planned."
**Careful.** This is the largest alternative by volume and the easiest to strawman. Respect it.

### 2. Free YouTube programs

**Why they pick it.** Free, credible faces, infinite supply, no signup.
**Real strength.** Production quality and coaching personality we cannot match on volume.
**Where it fails them.** Nothing is logged, so nothing progresses. A twelve-week program on a
playlist has no idea what you lifted in week three. Finding the right video each session is its
own chore.
**What we say.** The difference is progression and memory, not content quality.
**Careful.** Never disparage a creator. Many of our best-fit users found training through one.

### 3. The stitched stack (a logger plus a calorie app plus a meditation app)

**Why they pick it.** Each piece is best-in-class at its own job. Hevy or Strong for logging,
MyFitnessPal or Cal AI for food, Headspace or Calm for mind.
**Real strength.** Depth. Hevy's logging history and MyFitnessPal's food database are genuinely
better than ours at their one job.
**Where it fails them.** Four logins, four habits, four notification streams, and no shared
picture. Nothing in the stack can tell you that your protein was low on the weeks your lifts
stalled.
**What we say.** "One app instead of five," and the dashboard as proof.
**Careful.** This is the alternative we position against primarily. Naming each app's real
strength earns the right to name the stack's limit. Do not claim we beat any of them at their own
job, because we do not.

### 4. An in-person trainer

**Why they pick it.** Accountability, real coaching, someone who notices when they do not show up.
**Real strength.** A human who adapts in real time and holds them to a standing appointment.
**Where it fails them.** Cost, scheduling, geography, and it stops the moment the sessions stop.
**What we say.** "A coach's structure without the standing appointment." Structure, not
replacement. We do not claim to replace a human coach.
**Careful.** Never quote a trainer price as a comparison anchor unless it is sourced and dated,
and never imply Become costs less, because Become has no price. The argument is schedule and
access, not money.

### 5. A general AI chatbot

**Why they pick it.** Instant, conversational, free, and it will write a plan in ten seconds.
**Real strength.** Speed and flexibility. It answers any question in any format.
**Where it fails them.** Nothing persists. No video, so form is on them. Nothing is logged, so
next week starts from zero. No progression model, so week four looks like week one.
**What we say.** Persistence and proof: the plan remembers you, the movement shows you, the week
gets written back to you.
**Careful.** Our own generator is AI. The argument is not "AI bad," it is "a plan that persists
and logs beats a plan that evaporates."

## How to use this map

- **In the canvas.** Rows 1 through 5 go straight into the alternatives component.
- **In messaging.** Each value theme must beat at least one row here. A theme that beats none is a
  feature description.
- **In ads and landing copy.** Name the alternative's real strength before its limit. That is the
  move that earns credibility, and it is the pattern the strongest competitor creative in
  `marketing/inspo-analysis.md` uses: sell the mechanism, not a promise.
- **Never** publish a comparison claim we have not verified this month, and never quote a
  competitor price without a checked date. See `competitor-analysis`.

## Validating this map

Fastest path from assumption to evidence, in order of cost:

1. Mine one-star and three-star reviews of the stack apps for recurring complaints. Free, fast,
   and it produces verbatim customer language for `become-context` section 9.
2. Read the subreddit threads where people describe their current setup. Look for the phrase
   "I use X for A and Y for B."
3. Ask Jon what his clients actually had installed when they started.
4. Ask five recent signups the one question that matters: what were you using before, and what
   were you using it for?
