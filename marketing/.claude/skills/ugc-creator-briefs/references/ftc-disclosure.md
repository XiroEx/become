# FTC Disclosure

Not legal advice. It is the operating standard we hold every Become creator deliverable to, drawn
from the FTC's endorsement guidance (Tier A source). When in doubt, disclose more prominently.

---

## When disclosure is required

A **material connection** between Become and the speaker must be disclosed. Material connections
include:

| Relationship | Disclosure required |
|---|---|
| Cash payment | Yes |
| Free or gifted product access | Yes |
| Affiliate commission or revenue share | Yes |
| Free access continuing after the campaign | Yes |
| Discount or perk in exchange for content | Yes |
| Employee or contractor of Become | Yes, and state the employment |
| Jon Don, the coach the app is built around | Yes, his relationship must be obvious |
| A member who paid nothing and got nothing, posting unprompted | No, but see the reposting rules |

There is no exemption for small creators, for organic-looking content, or for posts we did not
commission but did pay for afterwards.

## The standard: clear and conspicuous

Four tests. All four must pass.

1. **Placement.** In the content itself, where a viewer will see it without taking any action. Not
   only in the caption, not behind "more," not in a bio, not in a comment.
2. **Prominence.** Legible size, sufficient contrast, on screen long enough to read. Two seconds
   minimum for a short-form video.
3. **Timing.** Early. First three seconds of a video, first line of a caption. A disclosure after
   the pitch is too late.
4. **Language.** The same language as the content. Plain words, not jargon.

## What passes

- On-screen text in the first three seconds: `#ad` or `Paid partnership with Become`.
- Spoken once, plainly, in the first ten seconds: "Become is paying me to show you this."
- Caption's first line opening with `#ad` before any other text.
- Platform paid-partnership label enabled **in addition to** the above.
- For affiliate links: disclosure at the point of the link, not only in a bio.

## What fails

| Fail | Why |
|---|---|
| Caption-only disclosure | Autoplay viewers never see it |
| `#ad` buried in a block of hashtags at the end | Not conspicuous |
| "Thanks to Become for having me" | Ambiguous about payment |
| Disclosure at the end of the video | After the persuasion |
| Platform label alone | Not visible in every surface the video appears in |
| Bio disclosure | Not tied to the specific content |
| Grey text on a light background | Not prominent |
| "sp" or "collab" | Not plain language |
| Gifted access with no disclosure | Free access is a material connection |

## Platform placement

| Platform | In-video | Caption | Platform tool |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram Reels | Text overlay, first 3s | First line | Paid partnership label |
| Instagram Stories | Text overlay on the first frame of the sequence | n/a | Paid partnership label |
| TikTok | Text overlay, first 3s | First line | Branded content toggle |
| YouTube Shorts | Text overlay, first 3s | First line of description | Paid promotion checkbox |

If the same asset runs as a paid ad from our account, the ad format's own advertiser attribution
covers it. If it runs whitelisted from the creator's handle, the disclosure must be in the creative.

## Comments and replies

Disclosure obligations extend to what the creator says under the post. A creator who is paid and
answers "no, I just found it and loved it" in a comment has undone the disclosure. Brief them on
this explicitly.

## Our own accounts

- The Become brand account speaking about Become needs no disclosure; the account is the advertiser.
- Jon Don is the coach the app is built around. His relationship must be obvious from the content
  or stated. Ambiguity about whether he is an independent reviewer is not acceptable.
- Employees and contractors posting about Become disclose the relationship.

## Enforcement inside our process

- The disclosure requirement is written into the brief, with exact wording and placement.
- The approval checklist checks it before anything ships.
- A draft that fails disclosure gets a recut, not a caption patch, because caption-only disclosure
  is the failure mode.
- An asset that has already published without disclosure gets fixed or taken down the same day.
- No creator content is amplified in paid until the disclosure passes.
