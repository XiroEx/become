# Claims Boundaries

Jon is a real person. A claim in his mouth carries more weight and more exposure than the same
claim on a brand account. These boundaries are hard.

---

## 1. Client stories

**The rule:** never invent a client, a client story, a client result, or a personal anecdote. If
Jon has not confirmed it happened, it did not happen.

This is the most tempting shortcut in coach copy, because a specific story is the strongest thing
a coach can say. It is also the one that ends a coach's credibility permanently when it turns out
to be composite.

| Instead of inventing | Write this |
|---|---|
| "I had a client who lost 30 pounds" | "The pattern I see most is people quitting in week three." |
| "One guy messaged me last week and said..." | "The question I get most is what to do after a missed week." |
| "I've helped hundreds of people" | "I wrote the phases in this program." |

If a real story is wanted, it goes in the **Needs Jon's sign-off** bucket with the exact wording
proposed, and it does not ship until he confirms it, including whether the client consented.

## 2. Results and outcomes

Nothing may promise or imply an outcome.

| Banned | Safe |
|---|---|
| "You'll be stronger in 6 weeks" | "The program progresses over six weeks and the numbers are logged so you can see what moved" |
| "Lose the weight for good" | "Weight is one of the things you can track. What it does is up to a lot of things" |
| "Guaranteed progress" | "The plan is a sequence. Following it is the variable" |
| Any pound, kilo, inch, or percentage about a body | Behaviour numbers only: sessions logged, sets, days |

Numbers Jon may use: sessions in a phase, days in a week, minutes in a session, reps and sets,
what the app counted. Numbers he may not use: anything about a body or an outcome, anything about
how many people use the app.

## 3. Medical, injury, and pregnancy

**The rule:** decline to diagnose, refer to a qualified clinician, offer to work around it after.

The standard response, structurally verbatim every time:

> "I am not going to guess at that over a comment. Get someone who can actually look at it to look
> at it. Once you know what you are dealing with, send it to me and we will build the training
> around it."

Never, under any framing:

- Name a condition or suggest what it might be.
- Suggest a stretch, a mobility drill, a modification, or a load change for undiagnosed pain.
- Comment on medication, supplements, hormones, sleep disorders, or eating disorders.
- Give pregnancy or postpartum programming advice.
- Say "it's probably just," which is a diagnosis with a hedge attached.

Why it is non-negotiable: it is outside a coach's scope, it creates real liability for a named
individual, and the helpful-sounding version is the one that causes harm.

**Disordered eating.** If a comment or DM suggests restriction, purging, or compulsive exercise,
do not coach it and do not engage on the numbers. Respond with care, point to professional
support, and do not turn the exchange into content.

## 4. Pricing

Become is free today and no pricing exists. Jon's complete permitted statement:

> "Free today. Email link, no card."

He may not say, hint, or joke about: a future price, an early-adopter rate, a discount, a
founding-member tier, a trial length, or a comparison to what anything else costs.

## 5. Product truth

He may only describe what the app does today: Dashboard, Training with coach-built phases, an AI
session and program generator, exercise demo videos, set logging with PR history, LIVE camera rep
counting, Nutrition with whole-plate photo logging, barcode scan and personal targets, Mind with
short guided sessions and mood tracking, Progress with weight and strength trends and the weekly
recap. Signup is an email magic link, no credit card, free today. Web push notifications exist.

Anything else: "not available today." Never a roadmap promise in his voice.

## 6. Bodies

- No before and after.
- No commentary on his own body or anyone else's.
- No aesthetic goal presented as a promise.
- No camera-roll photos of himself. Anything he appears in is filmed for the post.
- No physique-based credibility. His programs and his reasoning are the credential.

## 7. Competitors

He may say what Become does. He may not rank a named competitor negatively, allege anything about
their data practices, or claim superiority he cannot demonstrate. Generous about them, specific
about us.

## 8. Other people's data

Never a screenshot of another user's account, message, log, or progress. Never a member's face or
body without written permission. Never a minor.

---

## The sign-off bucket

Every deliverable ends with a list, even when empty. It contains anything that asserts:

- A fact about Jon's history, credentials, or practice.
- A client, a client outcome, or a story.
- A statement about how a program was designed or why.
- An opinion strong enough that he would want to have said it himself.
- Any number at all that is not a product behaviour.

Send the list with the copy. Nothing in it ships until he confirms it. "Nothing needs sign-off"
is a valid line. Omitting the section is not.
